## Why it will be good

### 1. Holdout results

Two boost sizes were tested once each. Each cell is the log-ratio vs. production, with the 95% CI and the number of companies where EASE won.

| Metric | EASE without boost | EASE with boost, c = 4 |
|---|---|---|
| View-through, 7 days (main) | +0.035 [-0.005, +0.075], 55 of 86 | **+0.113** [+0.069, +0.151], 75 of 86 |
| View-through, 1 day | +0.049 [+0.006, +0.093], 49 of 86 | **+0.101** [+0.054, +0.145], 70 of 86 |
| Incremental response | +0.101 [+0.021, +0.178], 51 of 76 | **+0.139** [+0.081, +0.199], 61 of 76 |
| View-through conversion, 7 days | -0.110 [-0.209, -0.012], 31 of 80 | +0.053 [-0.020, +0.125], 45 of 75 |
| Incremental conversion | +0.052 [-0.047, +0.153], 42 of 68 | +0.021 [-0.087, +0.115], 46 of 68 |

- c = 8 matched c = 4 on view-through and trailed slightly on incremental response.
- Conversions are closed to a small lead, but the CI includes zero.

<Note>
  The no-boost column comes from a regrade with newer grading code, so the c = 4 vs. no-boost gain carries extra noise.
</Note>

### 2. Backwards ranking

For a fifth to a quarter of companies, production ranks its least responsive households highest on incremental lift.

| Window | Production | EASE |
|---|---|---|
| Tuning | 19 of 74 (26%) | 2 of 74 |
| Holdout | 15 of 76 (20%) | 3 of 76 |

### 3. Cause of the conversion gap

A "lapsed visitor" visited a company more than 90 days ago but not since. These convert well. Production ranks them high because a past visit raises its score. EASE ignores a household's own past visits when scoring that company.

Share of lapsed visitors in the top tenth:

| Production | EASE today | EASE with boost, c = infinity |
|---|---|---|
| 44.5% | 14.0% | 47.1% |

### 4. The visited boost

```text
score = EASE score + c × (company's largest EASE weight) × (visit weight, halving every 2 months)
```

- Any c ≥ 2 lifts the main metric to about +0.10 on average (July / August tuning), versus about 0 without it.
- c = 4 was the soak choice. c = infinity was dropped before the holdout because c = 4 matched c = 8 on the main metric and kept more incremental gain.

### 5. Simplicity and cost

EASE needs one matrix inverse over about 640 companies. A weekly run takes about 28 minutes on 10 CPU workers.

| | Cost per run |
|---|---|
| Production (GPU training plus scoring) | about $17.39 |
| EASE (added) | about \$9.99 (range \$8.70 to \$11.31) |

## How we do it

A single reviewed constant, `TVP_CHALLENGER_MODEL = 'ease'`, creates a separate Airflow DAG for the challenger, reusing production's scoring step.

<Tip>
  **Protections for production:** separate pipeline (no waiting behind production runs); one-hour timeout, then fall back to production's score; column check with fallback; old challenger tables deleted after each run; MLflow tracking without model registration.
</Tip>

## How we decide

**Lift** per company = (7-day view-through visits per impression in a slot) / (same in control) − 1.

The statistic is the median across companies of lift(challenger) − lift(production), in percentage points, computed for view-through and incremental lift. Only companies with at least 5,000 impressions in all three slots count.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Promote" icon="circle-check">
    Either metric's gain is established (CI above zero) and neither shows an established loss. Incremental gain alone is enough, but a promotion based on it alone is confirmed in a second soak.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Reject" icon="circle-xmark">
    Neither metric shows an established gain by the end, or either shows an established loss.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

- The +3 point view-through target is the smallest gain a one-month soak can establish at about 50 companies (±2.6 points). Incremental lift is noisier (±8 points), so it has no numeric target.
- The challenger gets one-sixth of traffic. A 1.4-point loss would lower a client's overall result by about 0.2 points, which is why the soak is cheap to start.
- Unscored companies are excluded from the read-out. The list is fixed weekly before results are seen.

## Appendix: How EASE works

EASE (Steck, 2019) learns a company-by-company weight matrix B that reconstructs each household's companies from its other companies, so B's diagonal is zero. It comes from one matrix inverse:

```text
P = (XᵀX + λI)⁻¹
B[i][j] = −P[i][j] / P[j][j],  B[j][j] = 0
score = u · B
```

- Production uses a 12-month user vector, weighting each visit by 0.5^(age/2) with age in months. A one-month window left 41% of households with no visits, all tied at the same score.
- Production uses λ = 250 on real counts. The original page's toy example uses a smaller scale.
- Because the diagonal of B is zero, EASE scores already-visited companies only from other companies, which is why it ranks lapsed visitors low and why the boost is needed.
